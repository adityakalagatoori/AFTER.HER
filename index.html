The Archive

A crime can last minutes.
Its consequences can last decades.

This archive exists to ask a question society rarely sits with: once the headlines fade, who remains to carry what happened? The case records here are real and sourced — documented through court judgments and named journalism, never invented. The chapters below are a reading lens for approaching them, not a description of any one person. Nothing redacted, timestamped, or quoted in this archive has been fabricated — where a detail isn't reliably sourced, the record says so plainly instead of filling the gap.

Why Does One Case Remain, and Another Disappear?

Not a ranking of suffering — a comparison of two real, documented cases and how differently the record retained them.

Remembered

"Nirbhaya," 2012

Sustained national protest for weeks. Directly produced the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013 — new offenses, longer sentences, a redefinition of sexual assault still in force today.

Underreported

Hathras, 2020

Widely covered at first, including the forced night cremation. By the March 2023 verdict — three acquittals, no rape conviction — national attention had receded far below what the crime itself drew.

One documented comparison, not a statistical claim about all cases. Full sourcing in each case file above.

Beyond These Six Cases

These six cases sit inside a much larger, slower system.

~90% of rape trial cases before Indian courts remained pending at the end of 2024.
24–27% has been the approximate conviction rate for completed rape trials across most of the last two decades.
~84 rape cases were registered with police per day across India in 2024 — roughly one every seventeen minutes.

Source: NCRB-derived reporting via Deccan Herald and Factly. Figures describe national trends, not the six cases profiled above individually. Conviction-rate figures reflect cases concluded within a given year, not a cohort's eventual outcome.

After the Headline

We talk about the moment.
We rarely talk about the years that follow.

How families carry weight they never asked for — sometimes becoming the quiet infrastructure of recovery, sometimes becoming another thing to recover from.
Trials that take years — the data section above shows how long, on average — and outcomes that rarely resemble closure.
Public attention arrives fast and leaves faster, as the Nirbhaya/Hathras comparison above documents directly.
The private, unphotographed work of continuing — a self that has to be rebuilt on terms no one else can see or grade.

Case Index About

The Case Index documents real, publicly adjudicated Indian legal cases, identified only by established case-names and sourced to named journalism and court records (cited within each case file). No personal survivor information beyond what is already public record is reproduced here, and no document, quote, timestamp or statistic on this site is invented. The reading-lens chapters, timeline labels, and systems diagram are a conceptual framework and do not describe any specific individual.

    Sharing Safely

    This prototype does not yet collect any stories. A future version of this archive would prioritize, before anything else:

    • Anonymity by default, not as an afterthought.
    • Informed consent at every stage, revocable at any time.
    • A clear, simple process for removal requests.
    • No collection of unnecessary personal information.
    • No public identification without explicit, written consent.
    • Visible links to safety and support resources.

    The Silence Archive

    This section of the archive would hold cases that never reached public attention — not because they mattered less, but because attention is unevenly distributed, as the Nirbhaya/Hathras comparison above documents. No such records exist in this prototype yet. A future version would gather them with the same sourcing standard as the six cases already here — verified first, published second.
